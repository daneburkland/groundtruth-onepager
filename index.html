The Problem— 01

America's land records are public — but practically unusable.

Every piece of land in the U.S. belongs to a county, and each of the 3,000+ counties keeps its own records, on its own website, in its own format. The information is free and public, but comparing properties means visiting county sites one by one. The companies that have already stitched it together sell it through expensive enterprise contracts — out of reach for individual investors, small firms, and new software tools.

How It Works— 02

An automated pipeline turns raw county data into answers.

01

Collect

Software connects directly to counties' public mapping systems and pulls every property record — no manual data entry.

02

Check

Each new source passes quality gates before it's trusted: is the data real, complete, and correctly labeled?

03

Standardize

Thousands of local formats become one consistent record: owner, address, size, value, taxes, and exact boundaries.

04

Enrich

Federal datasets are layered on top, answering the question every buyer has: "Could I actually build here?"

✱
Field Note · The crew is AI

Expanding to a new state starts with two words — "Add Iowa." From there, AI agents do the legwork a team of researchers used to: they fan out across the state, hunt down each county's data portal, decode its one-of-a-kind format, and wire it into the pipeline. Then the automated machinery takes over — checking, standardizing, and enriching with no human in the loop. It's how a small team covers a country with 3,000+ counties.

The Enrichment Layers— 03

Insight no single county record contains.

Groundtruth cross-references each property against trusted federal sources — FEMA, the U.S. Census Bureau, USGS, and the USDA — so a single record tells you not just what a property is, but what it's like to own it.

Who It's For— 04

Two doors into the same data.

Anyone can use the free map explorer — click any parcel in a covered county and instantly see its full profile. Professionals and software builders plug into the data service (API), which lets their own tools ask questions like "find every parcel over 5 acres, outside a flood zone, assessed under $50,000."

Land investors

Screen thousands of properties for deals and spot red flags — flood zones, wetlands, steep terrain — before ever visiting a site.

Developers & builders

Compare candidate sites on buildability, ownership, and value in minutes instead of weeks of county-by-county research.

Software & AI tools

Built from day one so that apps — and AI assistants — can query land data directly, something legacy providers weren't designed for.

The Business— 05

Free to explore. Paid to build on.

"The map is the storefront. The data service is the product."

Browsing the map costs nothing and requires no signup — that's how people discover the data is good. Heavy, automated use is what costs money. The incumbents (Regrid, ATTOM, CoreLogic) sell roughly the same underlying public data through opaque enterprise contracts; Groundtruth's bet is that transparent, self-serve pricing — and being the easiest source for AI tools to use — wins the next generation of customers.
